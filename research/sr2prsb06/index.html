Sr₂PrSbO₆ double perovskite as a solar-blind UV photodetector

First authorHSE06Drift–diffusion TCADWide-gap oxide
Read the published article ↗Author manuscript ↗See the poster ↗

Why it matters. Solar-blind UV-C detection is useful when visible sunlight should not create background noise. This study carries Sr₂PrSbO₆ from first-principles structure and electronic properties into a self-consistent device model, showing how a wide-gap oxide can become a selective deep-UV detector.

The research question

Can a stable, lead-free double-perovskite oxide provide an intrinsic absorption cutoff inside the solar-blind UV-C window and retain useful device-level performance?

What I did

  • Computed the structural, electronic, optical, mechanical, phonon, thermodynamic, and transport properties of Sr₂PrSbO₆.
  • Used the HSE06 electronic structure to determine the absorption edge and optical response.
  • Coupled the material parameters to a metal–semiconductor–metal drift–diffusion device model.
  • Performed the calculations, data handling, plots, interpretation, and original manuscript drafting as first author.
5.090eV indirect HSE06 bandgap
243.6nm intrinsic optical cutoff
2.82mA W⁻¹ baseline responsivity

What the device model adds

A material bandgap is only the first step. The device model tests how optical generation, contacts, carrier transport, thickness, bias, and lifetime shape the measurable response. For the baseline device, the response disappears above the calculated cutoff and the photocurrent exceeds the dark current by more than thirteen orders of magnitude at 10 V.

Citation: M. Mohiuddin and A. Kabir, “Sr₂PrSbO₆ Double Perovskite as a Solar-Blind UV Photodetector: A Combined DFT and Device Simulation Study,” Journal of Materials Chemistry C, published 21 September 2026. DOI: 10.1039/D6TC02359B.

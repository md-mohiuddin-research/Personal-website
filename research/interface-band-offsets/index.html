Interface band offsets as the common limit across four lead-free perovskite absorbers

First authorCOMSOL MultiphysicsFull-wave opticsDrift–diffusion transport
Read the manuscript ↗

Why it matters. Lead-free absorbers are often compared through optimistic single-material device simulations. This study asks a more practical question: when four very different absorbers share one device architecture, what actually sets the voltage, current, and fill factor?

The research question

Does better absorber absorption automatically produce a better lead-free perovskite solar cell, or can interfaces and transport layers become the real bottleneck?

What I did

  • Built a unified finite-element COMSOL workflow coupling full-wave electromagnetic optics with drift–diffusion transport.
  • Compared BaZrS₃, FASnI₃, Cs₂AgBiBr₆, and Cu₂AgBiI₆ in one fixed architecture.
  • Tracked parameter provenance, swept uncertain inputs, and cross-checked optical energy budgets and photocurrent integration.
  • Performed the modeling, analysis, plotting, and first-author manuscript writing.

What the comparison shows

Under defect-free interfaces, optical collection is not the limiting step. The four absorbers split into two mechanistic classes: conduction-band cliffs at the electron-transport interface for FASnI₃ and Cu₂AgBiI₆, and valence-band cliffs at the hole-transport interface for BaZrS₃ and Cs₂AgBiBr₆. Transport-layer band engineering, rather than absorber optimization alone, becomes the operative design problem.

Citation: M. Mohiuddin and A. Kabir, “Interface Band Offsets as the Common Limit Across Four Lead-Free Perovskite Solar-Cell Absorbers: A Coupled Opto-Electrical COMSOL Study,” under revision in Advanced Electronic Materials.
